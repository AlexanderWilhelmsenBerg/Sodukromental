The four-slide opening and three sentence 4s carry into this expansion. The first amoeba remains the only fixed helper and favors 4. ## Opening slides and the number pad

**Opening.** Put “Slide one loose tile into a touching empty square. Tiles move up, down, left or right.” next to the starter board. While selected, show a short track only toward reachable gaps. A pressed tile follows the finger on the chosen legal axis by at most one cell. Choose the dominant axis after touch slop. Hold at the origin while the axis is ambiguous. A blocked direction, anchored tile, diagonal-only movement or a second tap on an unrelated square leaves the source in place. On release beyond the midpoint of a legal track, commit one slide. Otherwise snap home. Cancellation, a second finger or a screen resize before release cancels the gesture. No floating copy can be dragged across the grid. Tap source then an adjacent gap, a focused arrow key or the accessible action **Slide up into row 1, column 2** calls the same legality check. Display the actual destination coordinates for the selected tile.

Initially, the sentence above the board says “In this 4×4 puzzle, the 4 is missing. Can you find someone to help you?” Once all four gaps can take a 4, highlight the three printed 4s, let each be dragged or tapped into its own gap and issue exactly 1 DNA per token. Spend the 3 DNA to hatch the amoeba. Its held 4 can be accepted directly or selected for the remaining cell. The normal number pad opens with the 5×5. If the player touches the sentence early, show its next action without consuming a token. The [four-slide reference route](game-specification.md#verified-opening-board) remains a regression fixture.

**After the opening.** Anchor a 3×3 number pad immediately under the grid in reading order `1 2 3 / 4 5 6 / 7 8 9`. On an N×N board, positions above N are inactive. Each eligible number remains in the same place, shows available count in Colony, and is identifiable by value and state without color. Select an empty cell, then press a number with stock. One physical copy moves from that number to that cell. Keep the cell outline on the placed tile and clear any armed number. The next tap on another cell selects it without repeating the prior digit. A zero-stock value leaves the cell selected and offers **Request 6**, for example. The subsequent delivery updates stock. The player decides when to place it. A rejected placement returns the same tile and explains the visible cause. Notes cost no stock. Givens stay locked. Keep deliberate digit-to-cell drag, helper handoff and accessible placement as alternatives. In the ordinary Library the pad has unlimited entries for the supported alphabet and no requests.

**Layout.** Show a separate loose-piece pile at the grid edge while gravity is active. Tilt and the direction buttons move loose pieces within that play area. The number pad stays below the grid for every direction including upside down. The portrait frame stays fixed. Keep a zero-stock button discoverable, and expose row/column selection for dense boards. At 320 dp with 2× text allow vertical scrolling with straightforward access between selected cell and pad. During a drag let the viewport scroll if needed. While a building is in progress, pin a small silhouette and its fitted/required count beside the board objective so progress stays visible even when the habitat strip is below the viewport. At 768 dp place the habitat to one side and keep numbers under their board. Touch-transparent creatures, sheets closed via Back and rule help must preserve square selection, notes, pad mode and focus. [Existing visual system](../design.md)

## Breaking the first completed 9×9

1. After the first classic 9×9 clear, pay its clear reward once and keep the solved board available to review. The explicit next action is **Break open the grid**. On press, owned workers walk in, test the seams, strike, loosen the dividers and lift recognizable numbered pieces. They carry frame sections and printed givens into place to assemble the authored Renban board. The player can tap or sweep a loose seam to cue an owned worker’s next strike. **Break remaining** cues all remaining strikes and finishes that sequence by tap, keyboard or screen reader. One worker can do the jobs sequentially. This is the first classic-to-variant transition in this run.
2. Freeze a source snapshot. Persist the transition ID, destination, eligible workers, outgoing physical tile IDs and destination ownership before playback. Allocate the nine copies of each value in a stable order to destination givens, worm captures and recoverable loose pieces. Reuse an outgoing ID once per copy. The old board remains a review snapshot with its solved placements. Its historic IDs are read-only there. Frame fragments are scenery. Numeral fragments are playable resources.
3. The long Renban worm eats the designated loose copies (one 2, two 4s, one 7 on the first board). Its body bends through the actual clue geometry. The rest settle toward gravity. From the pile the player can drag a loose digit onto its numbered pad slot **or directly onto an eligible puzzle cell**. Tap a piece then **Sort into pad** or select a target and choose that piece for an equivalent accessible action. A wrong pad slot bounces the same piece back. The active grid accepts direct placements as each corresponding area opens. **Skip animation** makes all eligible targets available immediately. A cell drop uses ordinary placement/undo. Allow **Sort remaining** in one action. The workers install authored givens and frame only. The player places every deduction. Requests recover the captured copies through the normal supply system.
4. Present the worm’s teaching example before the player needs its rule. **Skip animation** assembles the identical board and leaves the same loose pile, counts, capture IDs, instructor and sorting choices. Replaying the lesson or interrupting before/after sorting cannot break the same board again, charge a second reward, restore a spent shard or double a capture. Save a partial sort and any first placement. Reduced motion uses still frames of the workers opening the frame, fitting givens and offering the remaining pile.

For each digit `d`, exactly nine live physical copies span givens, placed loose copies, unsorted pile, available stock, reserved/in-transit/offered copies, collector stash and outstanding worm captures. A reservation or offer is one ownership state. Retrieval transfers the captured copy into delivery with its original ID. Assert the equation after each route, undo, sort-all, skip and cold resume. Use the authored Renban givens, constraints and unique solution as the destination fixture. An explicit **Retry puzzle** restores the destination from the same saved allocation, including the unsorted/stock and worm-capture split, without replaying the source clear. A direct-drop undo returns the physical piece to its pile. An expired helper offer returns it to the pad. Salvage records one tombstone and one replacement with the same provenance, never two live copies. Treat persisted Renban progress (including an unopened-again route) or any later clear as proof the boundary was crossed. A legacy save with a cleared classic 9×9 and no Renban progress may enter the transition. Any save already beyond it keeps its board and can replay the demonstration through **?**.

## Rule teachers

At the first encounter with each rule, show a named creature, one sentence in plain language, and an isolated worked mini-grid with a valid and a counterexample. The Renban worm is the clue itself. An owned helper introduces other performers or demonstrates their clues. Provide **Try an example**, **Play** and **? Rule help**. The example uses its own board and resources. Entering a wrong example answer explains the constraint and allows another try. Save first-seen and practiced separately. Skip, reduced motion and sound-off still deliver the written rule and marked squares. **?** reopens the example at any time, including from combined boards. On combined boards list every active rule and give each its own example. For a setter's custom variation the author’s wording and declared exceptions take precedence. Offer replay from the field guide after prestige.

| Encounter | Creature action and worked example |
| --- | --- |
| Renban | The long worm stretches over four cells and says “My whole line uses consecutive digits, once each. They can be in any order.” Show `6,4,7,5`. `4,6,7,8` has a gap. On a four-cell practice line `2,5,3,?`, the missing digit is 4. Keep its distinct line pattern after it settles. |
| Thermometer | A worker warms the bulb. `2,5,8` rises from bulb to tip. `2,5,4` cools at the last cell. |
| White and black Kropki | Two beetles spin the white dot for `3–4` (one apart), then roll the black dot for `3–6` (one doubles the other). `2–4` fails white and `3–5` fails black. Explain any all-dots-given condition only when the board declares it. |
| German Whispers | A helper walks a ridged line. `2–8` differs by six and works. `2–6` differs by four and fails on a 9×9. |
| Killer cages | A builder hangs a 7 on a two-cell cage. `2+5` fits, `3+5` exceeds it. Cage digits do not repeat. |
| Arrows | A courier carries `2` and `4` toward a circle containing `6`. A `5` in the circle would give a different total. |
| Palindrome and Region Sum Lines | One helper reads `2,5,2` from both ends. Another crosses a region boundary carrying `1+4` on one side and `5` on the other. `2,5,3` fails the mirror. `1+4` opposite `6` fails the equal sum. Each segment is a contiguous portion of the line between region boundaries, with endpoints as applicable. Show both lines separately in a finale. |
| Sandwich and X-Sums | A baker shows `1,2,3,9` with a sandwich total of 5 between the crusts. A scout shows an edge starting `3,1,5`. The leading 3 tells it to add three digits, giving a clue of 9. A sandwich clue of 6 fails the first example, and an X-Sum clue of 6 fails the second. |
| Diagonal and Anti-Knight | A helper traces a long diagonal and objects to a repeated 4. A hopping creature leaps two across and one down from a 4 and shows why the landing square cannot also be 4. |
| Halo, Trail and Linked fog | An owned worker strikes a torch and steps into a practice patch. Show one placement that reveals an orthogonal neighbor patch in Halo, one that follows an authored Trail, and two marked cells acting together in Linked. Show the same demo with a wrong digit or only one of two Linked prerequisites, and let the player see the patch stay dark. In Night Market show the lit dot and its two visible cells before the Kropki explanation. |
| Gravity and mixed expeditions | The amoeba braces beside a given as loose pieces fall toward the low edge. Offer the direction-button equivalent. A mixed expedition names all active rules. Its examples use visible information and never disclose unrevealed clue geometry. |

## Owned torches

Fog begins dark. Choose an eligible **owned** worker and show it walking from its home, striking a small flame and carrying that flame into the starter patch. On arrival, reveal exactly the authored cells and clues. A correct trigger journals an earned patch. A different available resident takes up a torch and walks to its handoff or post. A wrong value, an already active trigger or replaying instructions never generates a flame. Reveal by authored trigger groups, not by arbitrary empty cells. Light already placed on a post remains after the guard returns. Neighboring unearned patches stay dark. Use the [same neutral fog and feathered boundary as the native Library](phase-2-sudoku-library.md#fog-palette-and-visible-edges). Before the first torch there is no lit edge. Once a patch lights, a crossing line may show a faint direction at its edge. The torch glow follows the worker and leaves adjacent covered digits and text completely concealed.

| Encounter in an ordinary new run | Required staffing and what the player sees |
| --- | --- |
| Halo | One starter. The first earned new patch introduces a second bearer and the handoff. The first worker guards until the second lights its torch. Subsequent patches rotate the two or dispatch more available workers. |
| Trail | Three distinct workers can staff the lead, handoff and trail post. Queue earned patches in stable order while active posts are busy. Release a worker after the next safe handoff. |
| Linked torches | Three workers form the relay when its marked prerequisites are satisfied. One correct placement toward a two-cell trigger shows progress and preserves the existing light. |
| Night Market | Four workers cover up to four posts after civilization. Earned lights illuminate the authored patch, including any visible Kropki endpoints. |

Before entering, show **Torches 1/2** (or the applicable count), list actual names, and offer **Call back**, **Raise a helper**, or **Open village**. Let a player begin the Halo starter and return for more crew without losing the board. A shortfall preserves earned reveals and permits work on the visible grid. A body arriving from the village fills a real post. A worker can set its lit lamp on a hook to fetch a required digit or material between waves. Completing a wave releases its transient posts immediately. A new trigger never has to fire to release the only available digit carrier. Every owned worker including the amoeba is eligible for basic torch work from the starter reveal. No unnamed duplicate can materialize to finish a job. Use distinct people for simultaneous posts and a different bearer for the next handoff. The same crew can serve successive waves. On resume, apply each earned delivery once with the assigned ID, travel leg and patch. Journeys continue while optional animation is skipped. Provide the housing, credit and population recovery path specified in the [colony rules](phase-2-colony.md#from-a-nest-to-a-civilization).

**Compatibility.** The intentional One Helper prestige charter uses the original amoeba to carry the same torches in sequential legs with stationary lamps. Returning charter visitors may react but cannot work. Preserve this accessible solo route for a pre-expansion fog board already in progress and for an existing pinned Colony challenge. New ordinary authored runs follow the staffing table. In standalone Library play, use the setter’s fog rules and reveal triggers directly, without colony staffing. An imported challenge can opt into crew mechanics only if its verified adapter can reproduce those same puzzle reveals. Hand Orders still prevents automatic digit restocking. No Producers still prevents producer purchases. Neither restricts population growth or torch work.

## Architecture and migration

- **One command path.** Route finger, tap, keyboard and screen-reader actions through the same board commands. `Campaign.slide` already checks orthogonal adjacency, locks and gaps. Constrain the gesture preview in the shared board view. Keep selection, armed source, note mode and visible stock distinct so a stale selection never spends a digit. Preserve undo provenance for shard, tray and helper sources. [Board input](../lib/board_view.dart) · [Colony controls](../lib/colony_screen.dart) · [Campaign domain](../lib/campaign.dart)
- **One actor per job.** Give trips source/destination anchors, start/due, worker ID, cargo ID and event ID. One shared simulation clock emits a stable order. UI and Flame interpolate. Prioritize earned torches and committed recovery, then explicit player priority, then waiting manual work and construction, then automated restock and ambient activity. Age equal-class requests and break ties by stable ID. Clear an idle prank before reserving the same actor for work. Park or release reservations on board changes so a worker cannot appear busy in two places. A creature depicted carrying a letter must own that letter’s job. [Dispatch and board switching](../lib/campaign.dart) · [Existing sprite rig](../lib/creature_view.dart)
- **Version boundaries.** Extend the campaign snapshot (currently v2) with versioned ecology, resolved individual, bonds, population credits, project sockets, the shatter ledger and torch crew legs. Use an additive, separately versioned Library save for note layers, colors and payload identity. Preserve the shared board format where the rule geometry is unchanged. Perform migration atomically. Preserve original saves on failure. Upgrade existing 27-option starter recipes and later unlocked traits as legacy resolved individuals, preserving name, rig, seed, favorite, job, memory, charter, archives and paid perks. Restore a pending hatch as its existing command or allow a free explicit conversion. Never debit again. Mature saves receive benefits for prior milestones once and acquire communal capacity without replaying clears. ## Slices and review gates

All rows are **specified**, with implementation and runtime acceptance pending. The story, mechanics, UI/UX and game-builder reviewers each receive the slice ID, player journey, build revision and reproduction. The coordinator reconciles findings and returns a concrete change and retest to the owning agent. The two source researchers independently check player identity and download-path fixtures. A stage ends when its listed scenario passes. Keep discovery/balance observations separate from data-loss, unsolvable-board and false-offline defects.

**Decisions from the specification run.** The story and mechanics proposals meet in a choice of actions with a demonstrated job consequence. The UI reviewer found that the current preview lets a diagonal drag appear legal until release. The builder found that the habitat can display the first offered creature on another worker’s trip. Both have explicit acceptance tests above. New staffed fog is a deliberate change to normal campaign progression. The saved-run and One Helper exceptions preserve commitments made to existing players. The source researchers found that a featured badge alone cannot establish puzzle identity. Both featured and unfeatured editions import into Sodukromental’s own player. The setter’s page and CtC video have dedicated reference actions. These are the criteria for the *next* debug handoff.

# Phase 2 · Balancing the incremental campaign

**Design decision for the next debug build.** The first ending takes 16 authored boards. It should take real effort to deduce the late boards and choose useful upgrades. A person who plays for ten minutes, makes one deduction and closes the phone still comes back to that placement and the colony work it set in motion. The following are proposed balance settings. The income ledger is calculated from the current rewards. Human completion times are targets awaiting playtests. [Campaign and routes](incremental-campaign-specification.md) · [Creature paths and settlements](phase-2-colony.md) · [Board interactions and staffing](phase-2-expansion.md)

Helpers offer and carry digits. The player decides the placement. Favorites, friendships, grudges and inherited shapes change how work looks and which optional bonuses fire. Show the named worker, cargo and effect on its first qualifying trip. A missing favorite never withholds a required digit. A rule lesson, a hint, an argument and a wrong placement pay no farmable currency. Preserve the existing consent and recall controls for companions. ## The cost of progress

Use two currencies throughout. DNA competes between hatching, nurture and DNA production. RNA also pays for the production infrastructure. Digits are physical inventory and glyphs build structures. The Meeting Hall has a guaranteed source of its eight typed materials. It has no DNA or RNA entrance fee.

| Checkpoint on a first ordinary route | Cumulative DNA / RNA from mandatory rewards only | Decision the balance supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4×4 | 0 / 6 | The three tutorial DNA were spent on the amoeba. The first RNA producer costs 4 RNA. |
| 5×5 | 5 / 18 | First designed hatch costs 4 / 8. The two rank‑1 producers together cost 1 / 10. The guaranteed puzzle rewards alone cover all three exactly. Previews and the next board are free. |
| 6×6 | 11 / 34 | Ignoring batch income, the starter package leaves 6 / 16 at this clear. Hatch a second designed creature for 6 / 12 if there is a free bed, or buy rank‑2 RNA production for 0 / 8. The village’s first communal resident is free in either case. |
| 8×8 and 9×9 | 26 / 72 and 35 / 94 | The first three base hatches together cost 19 / 37. More bodies compete for housing and field posts. Nurturing a new response on a creature the player loves costs 1 / 2. |
| Arrows and first ending | 56 / 164 and 70 / 204 | The nine ordinary variants across the shared path and selected route pay 3 / 10 each. Its finale pays 8 / 20. Optional boards, producers and Library challenges are excluded from every row. |

For an actual producer-first run, buy the RNA producer after 4×4. At the 5×5 clear its first batch guarantees 6 extra RNA. Buy the DNA producer on a different digit and hatch the first helper. The wallet now holds **0 DNA / 6 RNA**. At the 6×6 clear the rank‑1 pair and puzzle rewards bring it to **8 / 28** before further purchases. With a free bed, the second base hatch and both rank‑2 upgrades together cost 8 / 32. The player is 4 RNA short at that moment and can take the 7×7 board immediately with any two of those purchases.

Keep the existing hatch curve and actual upgrade debits. Each target rank costs the full rank times its base price. Rank‑2 RNA costs 8 RNA and rank‑3 costs 12 RNA. Rank‑2 DNA costs 2 DNA plus 12 RNA. The shop shows the **incremental benefit** beside the full price. With one additional rank, an RNA producer earns six extra RNA per completed production batch, so either RNA upgrade recovers its marginal cost within two full batches. A DNA upgrade also consumes RNA and its quote must show both currencies. ### Let a solved puzzle finish the producers’ work

At the existing rates, one rank‑1 producer of each type earns 3 DNA and 12 RNA in 120 seconds. A standard lesson pays 3 and 10. Our proposed change keeps the rates and gives each producer a finite batch tied to authored progress. The player sees “4 produces RNA · 4 of 6 deliveries complete” and the next completion condition.

- The first clear of 4×4 opens the first batch. Each subsequent distinct authored first clear in the run settles the preceding batch, then opens one more. This includes optional authored lessons, finales and expeditions. A retry, board switch, practice clear, Library challenge or application restart cannot open another batch. Pending work can continue on the clock while the player solves elsewhere.
- Per batch, an RNA producer has six 10-second cycles and a DNA producer has two 40-second cycles. Each cycle pays its commissioned rank. A newly installed producer can join the current batch once at rank 1. Its timer starts on installation. A no-cost Warm Start producer follows the same rule. An existing producer’s rank upgrade takes effect from the next batch. Show that effective point before purchase. The two value slots, rank unlocks and one-producer-per-digit rule still apply.
- Ordinary elapsed play delivers units automatically, including a cycle exactly at its due time. On a new qualifying first clear, credit any units outstanding at the commissioned rank immediately. With one producer of each at rank 1, finishing a lesson after 20 seconds yields 0 DNA / 2 RNA from the clock, then 2 / 4 from settlement. Finishing after 100 seconds yields 2 / 6 from the clock and zero from settlement. Including the ordinary clear reward, both players earn **5 DNA / 16 RNA** for this interval. A player who leaves the game open all night earns at most the current batch. No collection tap is required.
- Account for at most two productive minutes per absence and the remaining cycles in the batch. Settle existing queued trips and population credits under their own rules. Preserve partial intervals, commissioned rank, delivered units, the batch’s opening clear ID and the last credited time. Commit batch settlement, clear reward and successor batch together. Rank changes, reloads and quick opening through prestige must not replay a payout. **No Producers** suppresses all batches, including Warm Start. For existing saves, settle old accrued work once under the prior clock cap, keep bought ranks and balances, then initialize one prospective batch. Archive eligibility and old clear IDs without retroactive batch payouts.
- Once a finale and the hall are both complete, settle the current batch immediately and record its ID. In this completed colony, settle each newly earned batch when it opens and settle any eligible first installation once. This lets the player spend the proceeds on final nurture before prestige. Opening or canceling the prestige preview issues no new batch. The reset preview shows the final run balance, its reset, and what the companion keeps.
- Keep the agreed **Play with Colony** reward of 3 DNA / 10 RNA once per eligible Logic Masters puzzle per run. Ten distinct solves therefore earn 30 / 100. This is a valid way to fund chosen creatures. It grants no campaign clear, producer batch, village arrival credit, route mastery or Legacy. Ordinary standalone Library play keeps its own progress and economy. The full authored route remains available offline without a download.

## Give the settlement a job on the board

Four independent, three-second base trips take 12 seconds with one worker and 6 seconds with two. A single pair carrier can deliver four copies of the *same* requested digit in two trips, also 6 seconds. It takes four trips for four distinct values. Show this difference on the next order. Nests and housing expand the roster. Concurrent work is limited to 2 village posts, 3 city posts and 4 civilization posts. Eight basic glyph trips for the hall take 24 worker-seconds, or 12 elapsed seconds with two dedicated workers and available materials. These are baseline dispatch fixtures on unimproved routes, excluding player input and speed traits. A completed material path gives a separate 3.0-to-2.7-second haul fixture. [Village paths and production](phase-2-colony.md#how-a-letter-becomes-a-village)

Add **three optional settlement orders** to teach allocation. Each is a saved, untimed task with one reward per run. Its reward is a named slot for an editable crew preset. A preset recalls assignment priorities and restock targets. It consumes ordinary available workers and obeys the charter. It grants no extra bodies or automated deductions. Show one order beside the settlement project, with its objective, reward and next eligible job. Players can leave it pending while solving. After accepting an order, progress can span eligible authored boards and their replays. For a player-requested delivery, a named worker can either fetch a missing copy or bring an existing, available copy from the pad. Keep the same tile ID through the trip and allow the player to take it back.

| Available | Completion event | Reusable reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Village | Place a worker-delivered copy of each of two different requested values on growth boards. For example, request a 7 on 7×7 and an 8 on 8×8. Save credit for the 7 when the board grows. | **Supply** recalls the chosen carriers and stock targets on the next board. |
| City | Install a beam on a house, storehouse, workshop or hall and fulfill a digit request while that project is underway. A reusable practice site with settlement-notice glyphs offers the same two jobs if the city’s projects are complete. | **Build** recalls a builder and the carrier priority the player chose. |
| Civilization, after learning Halo | On an authored fog board, light an earned patch, switch priority, and deliver a requested digit. An eligible worker can set down its lit lamp between jobs. | **Lanterns** recalls the torch and digit priorities. On a later expedition the player can switch back in one action. |

Persist completed order steps across sessions. A practice site reserves its own glyphs and grants no construction benefits. Each event counts once and a canceled reservation counts only after delivery. One Helper can demonstrate all three orders in sequence with the amoeba. Hand Orders presets recall role priorities and suggested values, leaving each request manual. Free welcoming, glyph notices and orders also work under No Producers. After prestige, archive order achievements and reset the run’s preset slots.

At the first prestige, 3 Legacy can buy Extra Nest for 3 or Warm Start for 2 with 1 banked. Preview the next meaningful change for the companion the player chose. Show the effective ETA for each Shorter Routes purchase after trait stacking and the 50% travel-time floor. For a 3-second digit trip with Chase and a qualifying niche, Legacy rank 2 yields 1.584 seconds. Rank 3 costs 4 Legacy and yields 1.500 seconds, an 84-millisecond gain on this trip. Display that gain and a basic carrier’s 2.4-to-2.1-second comparison before purchase. [Legacy costs and charters](incremental-campaign-specification.md#prestige-reset)

At fog entry, show the real crew count, names and the recovery action in the board view. Ordinary Halo needs 2 workers, Trail and Linked need 3, and Night Market needs 4. An amoeba-only village gains a second body with its founding credit, a third from the 7×7 clear and a fourth from the 8×8 clear. Paused welcomes bank at most two credits. Spend banked credits into free communal beds and call back busy workers when a post needs them. If a player deliberately defers all welcomes, opening Halo with one body and two banked credits can immediately supply its second body. Subsequent first clears and the existing one-credit-per-minute clock support the optional larger crews. Display any remaining shortfall before attempting a handoff. One Helper uses sequential lamps. New standard runs use distinct residents. [Population and housing](phase-2-colony.md#from-a-nest-to-a-civilization) · [Torch rules and legacy saves](phase-2-expansion.md#owned-torches)

## Incremental agent and coordinator handoff

For each slice, the **incremental balance agent** receives the current reward table, fixed board IDs, relevant charter and a before/after save. Its standing brief is to compare one directly relevant mechanism with a dated or pinned first-party source, calculate who benefits and what they give up, replay a no-spend and an optimized path on paper, identify a waiting or repeat-reward exploit, and return one proposed change with a numeric acceptance test. Reuse checked research unless a design decision changes. The agent recommends. The coordinator accepts or revises a change, records the reason and sends the same failing case back for retest.

| Slice and dependency | Owners | Evidence required for the future debug handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 · Rewards and production, after the opening and village design | Incremental agent + game builder | Reproduce the ledger, 20s/100s parity, old-save conversion and all charters. Compare long idle, fast play, reopening, rank upgrades, canceled hatch and 10 distinct Library challenges. Each balance and commissioning event must reconcile. |
| E2 · Work and settlement orders, after E1 and settlement/fog slices | Mechanics + story + UI/UX | For each order show its first real contributor, the task the player gives up, its effect on the board and a reachable next action on a phone. Run the one- and two-worker fixtures, all three charters, paused welcomes and a spend-all fixture. The builder checks reservation ownership, replay and migration. |
| E3 · Difficulty and prestige, after E1/E2 | Coordinator + incremental agent | Trace every finale’s joint deduction and run all 16 mandatory boards with free recruits, zero passive income and no Library content. Repeat with chosen specialists and with a retained companion. Check the last-batch settlement in both hall-first and finale-first orders, remaining expedition batches, prestige preview, retained identity and the actual next-run upgrade benefit. |

Recruit six first-time campaign testers who know basic Sudoku, with a mix of new and experienced variant solvers. Record assisted and unassisted paths separately. Observe the opening and one matched colony decision live, then collect per-board active minutes, stalls, purchases and the point each tester chose to stop. The debug review records ranges and individual exceptions. Validate these tuning hypotheses in the debug playtest.

- **Difficulty.** At least four of six should explain the linked deduction on their finale using visible clues, with rule help available. If players breeze through late boards, revise the deductions first. If they cannot identify a visible next step, fix the clue sequence or lesson. Never charge for a hint.
- **Reward and attention.** At least four of six should predict which of speed or paired cargo helps a shown queue, see their choice affect the next delivery and name one reason to keep their creature. If they cannot, revise the work demonstration and preview before raising prices. Observe whether a second preset sees voluntary reuse.
- **Waiting.** Time only a known, actionable deduction for which supplies or a torch block all progress. Target a median of zero and a 95th percentile at or below five seconds. Investigate every longer involuntary stall against the existing five-second design limit. Prioritize the needed job or show an immediate recovery. Keep thinking time, optional building and time away separate.
- **Return play.** Invite the same testers to stay in their completed colony or preview a reset. Record the actual choice and reason before discussing incentives. Follow a returning player to the first occasion when their chosen charter, permanent purchase or companion changes a decision. If returning players only recall the old answers, revisit the colony decisions and route signposting before extending the run.
- **Stop for correction** if a profile can buy its way past an authored deduction, a charter or poor purchase strands the main route, an identical batch pays more for waiting than for a prompt clear, or a reload duplicates a payout. Target first-prestige time by tuning authored board difficulty once those checks pass. Report observed times and any misses with the debug handoff.

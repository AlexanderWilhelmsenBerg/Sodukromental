# Phase 2 · The working colony and its next founder

**Proposed design for the next debug cycle.** At the village, a player who has just deduced a 7 can ask for one urgently or ask for four and go back to making notes. By the end, that same player can recognize who lifted the frame of the first grid, who lit the market and whose habits they want to take into a new colony. Make those choices affect real work. Judge the difficulty by the deductions the player can explain.

Keep **13 shared boards + 3 boards on one chosen route = 16 to the first ending**. The other six route boards, two fog lessons and four expeditions bring the catalog to **28**. One finale and the eight-piece Meeting Hall complete the colony. Each is available to finish first. New design here consists of board-specific scenes, a single optional work goal, on-hand digit deliveries for settlement orders, worked mixed-rule examples, and an archive comparison for a returning player. The figures below are paper fixtures and playtest targets. ## Work worth repeating

**Pin one goal** in the existing Colony sheet, such as “Raise my third helper”, “Give Reed a roof trade” or “Finish the market lantern”. Show the existing quote, the missing resource or socket, and up to two immediately applicable sources. A player aiming for a hatch can see which next authored clear will cover the shortfall or select an eligible downloaded Colony challenge. A pinned lamp pattern can point to the missing Trail lesson or the next material. Pinning, replacing and clearing this reminder cost nothing and leave the underlying jobs under player control. Refresh prices after other spending. Clear the pin on completion or archive. An affordable goal waits for the existing confirmation.

**Example budget.** From the 6×6 producer-first wallet of 8 / 28, hatch the second designed helper for 6 / 12. Keep both producers at rank 1. At the 7×7 clear add 7 / 18 from the board and 2 / 6 from its production interval, for **11 / 40**. At the 8×8 clear add 8 / 20 plus 2 / 6 for **21 / 66**, assuming no further income or spend. A third base hatch with a free berth takes 9 / 17. Two distinct, eligible Workshop lessons for two residents with empty roof response slots instead cost 2 / 4 in total, leaving **19 / 62**. A rank‑2 RNA installation costs 0 / 8 and adds six RNA per subsequent full commissioned batch. Present these as competing uses of the same wallet. Show when the hatch needs a berth and when a lesson needs a completed Workshop.

The third hatch pin also gives a player a reason to choose their next puzzle. Immediately after the second hatch at 6×6, the available 2 / 16 leaves a shortfall of 7 DNA and 1 RNA for a 9 / 17 hatch. The next campaign clear with the rank‑1 pair covers it. A player who prefers new Library puzzles can instead clear three distinct eligible challenges for 9 / 30, reaching 11 / 46 before any timed production. Two such challenges alone leave them one DNA short. Stop recommending income as soon as the price is covered. If the authored boards are exhausted, show only genuinely unclaimed sources and the ability to unpin.

**Give a trade a shift of real work.** Queue one resident’s roof lesson for 1 / 2, then send that resident with a support on an eligible build. A basic, unmodified support trip takes 3 seconds before training and 2.1 seconds with Fit the supports. Train a second resident with Read the settlement notice, then compare their next notice trips. The support fitter still takes 3 seconds on an ordinary beam. The notice reader takes 2.1 seconds on a notice beam. The player now knows which worker to keep on the next project. Use a pending build or Build’s practice site when appropriate. A full response family ends that training decision. A canceled lesson releases its reservation, a completed lesson spends once, and repeating a demonstration brings no payment. This loop can serve another eligible resident as the roster grows. The project, habit or hatch named on the pin tells the player when to stop.

## An ending with a deduction the player can explain

On the map, a route row lists its two lessons, progress out of three and **Resume** for an unfinished board. Choosing a route starts or resumes its next eligible lesson. Returning to the map preserves entries, notes and cargo ownership. Time, paid training, hints used and number of switches have no bearing on the ending.

| Dedication | Two lessons and the finale’s worked connection |
| --- | --- |
| **Lines · Open the bridge** | A Palindrome carries one value to its mirror. A Region Sum Line balances all its region segments. On the finale’s line down column 2, R3C2 alone equals R4C2 + R5C2 + R6C2. The first two terms in that sum are distinct positive digits, and its total is at most 9. Therefore R6C2 is at most 6. The palindrome beginning there ends at R7C4, so the same upper bound crosses to R7C4. |
| **Outside Clues · Open the provisions counter** | Sandwich counts what lies inside the crusts. X-Sums uses the first digit to choose the number of terms. On the finale the column 4 X-Sum is 41. Six distinct digits sum to at most 39, a leading 7 is excluded by R8C4, and a leading 9 would sum to 45. Hence R1C4 = 8. At a worked bookmark with the first row’s crusts established at C1 and C7 and the entries C2 = 7, C3 = 2, C5 = 3, its horizontal sandwich total of 24 now fixes R1C6 = 24 − 7 − 2 − 8 − 3 = 4. |
| **Positions · Mark the crossing lanes** | Diagonal requires a complete set along each long ribbon. Anti-Knight rules out matching digits one jump apart. In the finale’s initial grid the main diagonal can contain 9 only at R3C3, R7C7 or R9C9 by ordinary unit exclusions. The visible 9s at R5C2 and R6C5 respectively rule out the first two by knight’s move. R9C9 must be 9. |

These are authored examples for hint authoring and post-clear review. Supply complete, reachable, visible-clue traces for all three in debug acceptance, including the placements leading to the Outside bookmark. For a separate Outside authoring check, enumerate first-row permutations against starting row, column and box givens. The horizontal sandwich total alone leaves seven rows. The X-Sum’s independently deduced R1C4 = 8 alone leaves sixteen. Their intersection is one row, `972834165`. Keep this enumeration in the fixture. Offer “where to look” before revealing the argument. The finale-first state leaves a dedication ready at the foundation. Hall-first shows the completed hall and the missing finale. The second completed condition records **one ending**, with actual Hall fitters and route contributors. The dedication decorates the same Hall at no additional material cost and opens with that ending. Subsequent route finales record their own route and first-time profile credit on archive.
